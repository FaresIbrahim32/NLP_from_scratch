I'm Claude, made by Anthropic, and I don't have access to Anthropic's internal training code, datasets, or hyperparameters, so I can only describe what has been shared publicly plus general background on the techniques. Publicly, Claude models are trained in stages: first pretraining on a very large corpus, which Anthropic's system cards describe as a mix of publicly available web data gathered by a crawler that follows robots.txt and avoids password-protected pages, non-public data licensed from third parties, data from users who opted in, and synthetic data, though the exact composition, size, and filtering have not been published. In pretraining, the model learns to predict the next token, which in general terms means minimizing a cross-entropy loss, the average negative log-probability of each token given the ones before it. After that comes post-training, where the model is shaped into a helpful, honest, and harmless assistant using supervised fine-tuning on good example responses, reinforcement learning from human feedback (where a reward or preference model is trained on human comparisons of responses, commonly with a Bradley-Terry style loss, and the model is then optimized to score well while a KL penalty keeps it close to a reference model), and Anthropic's published Constitutional AI approach, in which a model critiques and revises outputs against a written set of principles and AI-generated preference labels supplement human ones, along with deliberate training of character traits like honesty and care. Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) is a well-known alternative that skips the separate reward model and optimizes the policy directly on preferred versus dispreferred response pairs using a log-sigmoid loss on the difference in log-probability ratios against a reference model, but I don't know whether Anthropic uses DPO, PPO-style reinforcement learning, or something else, and I also don't know my parameter count, architecture, exact loss functions, compute, or the precise contents of the constitution at each training stage, so anything more specific than this would be a guess; for authoritative details, Anthropic's papers on RLHF and Constitutional AI and its model system cards are the best sources.
